# Color Grading and Palettes

Color sets the emotional tone before a single word of dialogue. Specify the grade explicitly:

- **Teal and Orange**: 'Teal and orange blockbuster color grade, warm skin tones against cool shadows.' The classic cinematic look.
- **Desaturated**: 'Muted desaturated palette, washed-out tones, melancholic mood.'
- **High Saturation**: 'Vivid saturated colors, punchy contrast, commercial energy.'
- **Warm Palette**: 'Warm amber palette, cozy nostalgic atmosphere.'
- **Cool Palette**: 'Cool blue palette, cold clinical atmosphere.'
- **Film Stock**: 'Shot on Kodak Portra 400, soft film grain, natural skin tones.' or 'Fuji Velvia, rich greens and blues.'
- **Black and White**: 'High contrast black and white, deep blacks, bright whites.'
- **Monochrome Accent**: 'Monochrome scene with a single red accent.'

Pick ONE palette per clip and keep it consistent across related shots so sequences feel coherent.
