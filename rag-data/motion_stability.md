# Motion Stability and Anti-Distortion

Distorted, 'melting' or warping video is almost always a motion problem. Prevention:

- **Slow It Down**: 'slow smooth motion, deliberate movement' renders cleanly. Fast, erratic motion breaks the model's frame-to-frame coherence.
- **Stabilize the Camera**: 'locked-off tripod shot' or 'smooth gimbal movement'. Handheld-shake language ('shaky cam') multiplies artifacts.
- **One Moving Element**: A single subject moving through a still environment is stable. Crowds, flocking birds and traffic streams tend to smear into abstract soup.
- **Keep Subjects In Frame**: Subjects entering or leaving the frame mid-clip cause identity morphing. Start with the subject already in frame.
- **Avoid Occlusion**: Limbs crossing in front of the body or objects passing in front of faces confuse structure tracking — prefer clear sightlines.
- **Structural Keywords**: 'anatomically correct, clear structural integrity, stable proportions, no morphing' reinforce coherence.
- **Environment Scale**: Distant wide shots of tiny moving figures lose coherence — bring the camera closer to the action.

If output still warps: reduce motion, simplify the scene, and shorten the action.
