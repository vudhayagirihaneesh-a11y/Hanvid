# Clean and Safe Output Guidelines

Disturbing output is usually caused by prompt language that pushes the model into its failure zones. Keep generations clean:

- **Avoid Horror Vocabulary**: words like 'disturbing', 'creepy', 'grotesque', 'mutilated', 'gory', 'eerie' do not create suspense — they summon deformed, unpleasant imagery. Describe atmosphere instead: 'tense, quiet, dimly lit'.
- **No Violence or Injury Descriptions**: impacts, wounds and struggle poses break anatomy and produce upsetting frames. Suggest tension through environment, not bodily harm.
- **No Body Horror**: extra limbs and melting faces are failures, not features — add 'anatomically correct, natural proportions' instead of describing deformity.
- **Neutral-to-Positive Framing**: professional studios describe what the camera sees in plain, observational language. Calm prompts produce calm footage.
- **Sensitivity Defaults**: keep content family-friendly by default; the pipeline reserves the right to refuse graphic requests.
- **When a client asks for 'dramatic'**: translate to 'dramatic lighting, stormy sky, intense expression' — drama through light and weather, never through harm.

A clean prompt vocabulary is the first line of defense for clean, comfortable video output.
